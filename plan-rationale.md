## 4. Analytical Framework

### Why sub-1:20 is a gamble (acknowledged)

- Riegel from 2:50 marathon → 1:19:xx HM equivalent, BUT
- Marathon was 2.5 years ago, before 2 years of ultra-focus
- Current road-pace fitness evidence (Sep runs) suggests honest HM shape of **1:28–1:30**
- Sub-1:20 requires 7 weeks to recover threshold capacity that normally takes 8–12 weeks

### Risk/reward trade accepted

User chose aggressive targets with explicit understanding that:
- Low-probability outcome of sub-1:20
- Real ceiling is likely a spring 2027 HM with proper threshold block
- Nov 15 is a reintroduction race, not the season peak
- Decision points along the way can scale targets honestly

## 5. Plan Structure (7 weeks)

```
Week 1 · Sep 28 → Oct 4 · Wake-up + diagnostic ·        68 km · 1 quality
Week 2 · Oct 5  → Oct 11 · Race week · 10K tune-up ·    49 km · 1 session + race
Week 3 · Oct 12 → Oct 18 · Reload + big threshold ·     72 km · threshold (Sat) + long run
Week 4 · Oct 19 → Oct 25 · Lab test + key long run ·    66 km · lab (Tue) + HMP long run (gate 3)
Week 5 · Oct 26 → Nov 1  · Business travel · down week · 74 km · easy + M/HMP long run
Week 6 · Nov 2  → Nov 8  · Sharpen ·                    72 km · 3 quality
Week 7 · Nov 9  → Nov 15 · Taper + HM race ·            44 km · 1 session + race
                                                       ────────────────────────
                                                       445 km total
```

### Design philosophy

1. **Threshold-heavy, not VO2-heavy** — HM PR comes from lifting T-pace, not from 400s. VO2 work introduced only Week 4+.
2. **Two quality days per week** from Week 3 onwards (Tue + Thu). Week 1 one-session re-introduction, Week 2 race-oriented.
3. **Easy days are actually easy** — 5:00–5:15/km, no drift. Lever that enables quality execution.
4. **Strides on 3+ easy days/week** — 5–6 × 100 m fast. Free neuromuscular adaptation, no injury risk. Critical for an ultra-adapted body.
5. **Long-run progression peaks at Week 4** (22 km with 8 km continuous @ HMP) — the aerobic-specific peak.
6. **10K race treated as training stimulus**, not a PR attempt — becomes HMP dress rehearsal.
7. **Lab test (Oct 20) treated as a hard session** — pre-shakeout + post-recovery built in.

## 8. Adjustments Made During the Block

### Early-plan corrections
- Initial 10K target 36:15 (Riegel) corrected after user pushback; recognized 2:50 marathon was stale (2.5 years, ultra focus since)
- Easy day volume raised 6–8 km → 10–12 km after user request for more volume
- Added second quality day (Thursday) starting Week 3 after user request for more threshold stimulus
- Removed Sun Sep 27 from Week 1 (started block Mon Sep 28)
- RPE 7 → 8 corrections: Week 2 Tue sharpener (3×2000 vs 5×1000 is physiologically harder), Week 6 Sun LR with 6 km HMP block

### Mid-block adjustments
- **Oct 25 session rebuilt** — original 24 km LR with 10 km continuous @ HMP was overcooked (equivalent to HM race effort inside a long run). Changed to 22 km with 8 km continuous @ HMP, RPE 9. Rationale: internal consistency with Week 5 KEY session; Pfitzinger-style benchmark.
- **Week 5 reorganized for lab test** — Thursday mixed session dropped to pre-test shakeout; Fri lab test treated as RPE 10 quality session; Saturday slashed from 13 km → 5 km; Sunday LR trimmed from 22 → 20 km. Volume drops 85 → 68 km, trade justified by calibration value of lab data.
- **Weeks 3–5 rebuilt around business travel (Sun Oct 25 – Thu Oct 29).** The trip was first announced for Oct 19–23, then corrected. Athlete can run easy daily during travel.
  - The 4×2 km @ HMP moved from Tue Oct 20 to Thu Oct 15. Week 3's two light threshold sessions were dropped, and the Sun Oct 18 long run stays at 4 km @ M.
  - The CV session (6×800) moved to Tue Oct 20, at home.
  - The 22 km / 8 km @ HMP long run moved from Sun Oct 25 (travel day) to Sat Oct 24, with a pacing cap (first km 3:50, ~310 W).
  - The Tue Oct 27 3×3 km @ HMP KEY was dropped. It falls during travel, and its HMP stimulus overlaps with the Oct 24 run, which is the stronger HM predictor. Gate 3 moved to Oct 24.
  - Oct 25–29 is easy running only. Thursday stays the pre-lab shakeout; the lab test (Oct 30) and the Nov 1 long run are unchanged.
- **Thu Oct 15 threshold stopped after rep 1 (sore legs).** Rep 1 was on target (3:49, 311 W, HR 185, matching Sep 29), so the limit was muscular soreness from the hilly 10K's descents, not fitness. The session moved to Sat Oct 17, conditional on Friday feeling normal. The Sun Oct 18 long run became 17 km all easy. Fallback if legs are still sore Saturday: run easy and do the 4×2 km on Tue Oct 20 in place of the CV. The 4×2 km is more specific to the HM, so the CV is the one to drop. Sat Oct 24 (gate 3) stays the top priority.
- **Sat Oct 17 threshold executed cleanly** — 3:48 / 3:47 / 3:50 / 3:47 at 315–317 W, HR 184–187, RPE 6. Same HR and RPE as the Sep 29 1 km reps, at double the rep length: threshold is improving. First session with every rep on the prescribed pace.
- **Lab test moved to Tue Oct 20.**
  - It replaces the Tue Oct 20 CV session; the ramp to exhaustion provides the VO2/CV stimulus. The CV isn't rescheduled, because Week 6 (Nov 3) already has one.
  - Mon Oct 19 is rest. Wed Oct 21 is a normal 10 km easy: a lab test (mostly sub-threshold steps plus 2–3 maximal minutes at the end of the ramp) costs about as much as an interval session, with 24–48 h recovery.
  - Sat Oct 24 (gate 3) is 4 days after the test, and the measured LT2 can be used to check the HMP target before it.
  - Week 5 (travel) becomes a down week: easy running plus the Nov 1 long run.
